# Terminal Redux — Operator Interview Kit

**Version:** 0.1 · 20 August 2026
**Target:** 12–15 interviews + 10 terrace observations, weeks 2–5
**Purpose:** close research open items #8, #9, #10, #11 · validate or kill W1 · produce a pilot candidate

---

## 0. What this round has to produce

Not "validation." Four specific outputs. If you finish the round without these, it didn't work.

1. **A go/no-go on W1** — the table-ageing / revenue-per-table-hour thesis, which is your top-ranked wedge and currently has *zero* supporting evidence.
2. **Real dwell and turnover numbers** for Serbian cafés. This data does not exist publicly. You are creating it, and it becomes a defensible asset in its own right — it's a slide in the deck and a paragraph nobody else can write.
3. **Answers to open items #9 (Wolt/Glovo commissions), #10 (normativi/KEP), #11 (staff theft prevalence).**
4. **A named pilot venue**, agreed in writing, with a start date.

A fifth output you'll get for free if you do this well: a list of the specific words operators use for things. Your UI copy should come from these transcripts, not from a dictionary.

## 1. Who to talk to

### The target list

Build it from **APR financial statements, NACE 56.10 (restaurants) and 56.30 (bars)**. Rank by revenue and venue count. Aim for ~30 names to land 12–15 conversations.

Mix you want:

| Segment | Count | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Independent café/bar, 1 venue, big terrace | 5 | Your volume market and the W1 thesis under its purest conditions |
| Full-service restaurant, 1 venue | 3 | Coursing, KDS, splitting — the complex end of the product |
| Multi-venue group, 3+ venues | 3 | Where the back-office and analytics story lives; also your best-paying customers |
| Splav / event venue | 1 | Extreme peak load, reservations behave differently, worth one data point |
| Specialty coffee | 1 | Least grey, most likely to care about normativi and consistency |
| Recently opened (<12 months) | 2 | The ~7,000/yr registration cohort — no incumbent habits, easiest to win |

Named candidates worth pursuing from the research: **Kafeterija** (~60 venues, BlackPeak-backed), **Walter** (60 restaurants, 25 cities), **CIG/Monument** (5th largest by revenue, runs its own delivery stack). These are unlikely to be pilot #1, but a conversation with any of them is worth three independents for calibration — and for the raise story, having talked to them matters.

### Two non-operator conversations that pay for themselves

- **2 × knjigovođa** serving hospitality clients. Thirty minutes each closes open item #10 (are normativi and KEP/KEPU books mandated, in what form) and gives you a real picture of the month-end re-keying that W6 kills. Accountants are also a distribution channel — an accountant who likes you recommends you to twenty venues.
- **1 × tax adviser** on napojnice, before you design anything tipping-shaped. Open item #3.

### Getting in the door

- **Walk in at 15:00–17:00 on a Tuesday, Wednesday or Thursday.** Dead time between lunch and evening. Never Friday, never during service, never a weekend.
- **Ask for the owner or manager by name** if you have it from APR. "Da li je [ime] tu?" gets a different reception than "can I speak to a manager."
- **Lead with the tablet, not with yourself.** *"Pravim sistem za kafiće, imam nešto da vam pokažem, treba mi 20 minuta i vaše mišljenje."* You're asking for a critique, not selling. That's a much easier yes, and it's true.
- **Buy something.** Sit down, order, wait for a lull. You're also collecting turnover data while you wait — see §4.
- **Expect ~1 in 3 to say yes** on a cold walk-in. Thirty doors gets you ten interviews; warm intros from the accountants get you the rest.
- **Leave a card with a URL.** Some of the best conversations happen when they call you back.

## 2. The interview

**45 minutes.** Record audio if they agree (*"samo da ne zapisujem sve"*) — you will not remember the vocabulary otherwise, and the vocabulary is half the value. Transcribe the same evening while you still remember the room.

**Structure: 15 minutes on their world, 20 on the tablet, 10 on close.** Do not invert this. If you demo first, everything after is politeness.

### Part 1 — their world (15 min). Never mention your product.

Open-ended, past-tense, specific. The rule throughout: **ask what happened, not what they think.**

1. "Provedite me kroz jedan petak. Od otvaranja do zatvaranja." *(Walk me through a Friday.)* Let it run. Interrupt only to ask "and then what happened."
2. "Koliko stolova ima bašta? Koliko unutra? Šta se desi kad padne kiša?" — this is W1's setup, and you want their unprompted account of the reconfiguration problem before you show them your answer.
3. "Kad ste poslednji put imali problem sa kasom — šta se tačno desilo?" *(Last time something went wrong with the till — what exactly happened?)* Past tense, specific incident. Not "what do you dislike about it."
4. "Koliko traje da obučite novog konobara?" and "Koliko ih je otišlo ovog leta?" *(Training time; how many left this summer.)* You're sizing W3 and confirming the staffing picture.
5. "Ko zatvara smenu i koliko traje?" *(Who closes the shift and how long does it take?)* The cash-up and reconciliation pain. Listen for whether it balances.
6. "Kako znate koliko ste zaradili juče? Gde to gledate?" — this tells you whether they look at anything at all, and on what device. Watch whether they reach for a phone.
7. "Šta radite sa Wolt i Glovo porudžbinama? Kako znate koliko vam ostane?" — open item #9, and the W8 pain. Ask directly for the commission percentage; some will tell you.
8. "Da li vas je neko kontrolisao? Šta se desilo?" *(Have you been inspected? What happened?)* This is W14, and the 57.9% closure rate says most of them have a story.

**On theft (open item #11) — handle carefully.** Never ask "do your staff steal from you." Ask instead: **"Da li vam se dešava da fali robe na kraju meseca? Koliko?"** *(Does stock come up short at month end? How much?)* — that's a neutral operational question, and the answer tells you what you need. Follow with "kako to proveravate?" If they volunteer more, let them.

**Listen for the words they use.** *Bašta, šank, normativ, manjak, popis, međuzbir, smena, pazar, kalo.* Write them down verbatim. Your UI should speak this vocabulary, not a translated one.

### Part 2 — the tablet (20 min). They drive; you don't touch it.

Hand it over. Give tasks, not a tour. **Say nothing while they work.** Every hesitation is data; every question they ask you is a design defect.

| # | Task, as you say it | What it tests | What to write down |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Sto 7 naručuje dve kafe i jednu rakiju." | Order entry speed, speed screen | Seconds to complete. Did they find items by grid or search? |
| 2 | "Sad ista ekipa hoće još jednu turu." | Repeat round — the highest-frequency bar action | Did they find it? Did they re-ring from scratch? |
| 3 | "Počela je kiša, cela ekipa sa sto 7 se seli unutra na sto 12." | **W1's core case** | Did they attempt it? Did they expect it to be possible? Face. |
| 4 | "Jedan od njih plaća 900 dinara i odlazi, ostali ostaju." | Arbitrary partial payment — impossible in mainstream systems | Watch for surprise. This is the moment that converts people |
| 5 | "Podelite račun na četvoro." | Even split with pre-filled amounts | Did they expect to type the amount four times? |
| 6 | "Podelite jednu flašu vina na troje." | Shared item across seats | Do they believe it's possible before trying? |
| 7 | *Point at the ageing indicator, say nothing.* "Šta mislite da ovo znači?" | **W1's comprehension test** | Do they read it unprompted and correctly? |
| 8 | "Šta biste hteli da vidite ovde a nema?" | Open-ended, at the end | Their answer here often outranks everything you planned |

**Time task 1 and task 5 with a stopwatch.** Comparable numbers across twelve venues are worth more than twelve impressions, and "median 19 seconds to a four-way split, across twelve venues" is a sentence you can use in a pitch.

### Part 3 — the close (10 min)

Only now, and only briefly, say what you're building: a Serbian POS, certified, works when the internet doesn't, made for terraces.

Then ask, in this order:

1. **"Šta biste morali da vidite da biste ovo probali?"** *(What would you need to see to try this?)* — their real objection, which is almost never price.
2. **"Šta plaćate sada, mesečno, za sve zajedno?"** — get the actual number including every module and the fiscal maintenance line. Anchors your pricing against reality, not against Cheers' price list.
3. **"Da li biste bili spremni da ovo probate besplatno, na jednom šanku, na šest meseci — ako ja budem tu prve nedelje?"** — the pilot ask. Ask everyone. Some will say yes and mean it.
4. **"Koga još treba da pitam?"** — referral. Hospitality in Belgrade is a small world; this is how you get from ten conversations to thirty.

## 3. The W1 kill criterion — decide it now, before you're attached

Write the threshold down before the first interview, because after ten conversations you will be able to hear whatever you want to hear.

**W1 passes if, across 12 interviews:**
- ≥6 operators, **unprompted**, read the table-ageing indicator correctly on task 7, **and**
- ≥4 ask some version of *"when can I have this"* or *"how much"* without being invited to, **and**
- ≥8 attempt task 3 (the rain move) rather than assuming it's impossible

**W1 fails if:** they read the ageing indicator as decoration, or they read it correctly and shrug. A polite "da, dobro je" is a fail. Interest that has to be explained into existence is a fail.

**If it fails,** the wedge ranking changes and you should re-rank against whatever they *did* react to — very possibly the partial-payment task, which is the most viscerally broken thing in every competing product. That's a good outcome discovered cheaply. It is much better than building a terrace-analytics product for a market that wants a till that splits bills properly.

## 4. The terrace counting protocol — open item #8

**No public data exists on Serbian café dwell time, table turnover or average check.** You're generating it. Ten venues × two hours each ≈ 20 hours of sitting, which you can partly overlap with waiting for interviews.

### Method

Pick **ten venues** across three bands: central Belgrade (Dorćol, Vračar, Skadarlija), residential (Novi Beograd, Zvezdara), and one or two in Novi Sad for a non-Belgrade control. Sit for **two hours**, twice per venue if you can — once on a weekday afternoon (14:00–16:00), once on a weekend evening (19:00–21:00).

Pick a **fixed block of 6–10 tables you can see clearly** and track only those. Trying to watch a whole terrace produces garbage.

For each table, record:

| Field | Note |
|---|---|
| Table ID | Your own numbering |
| Party size | |
| Seated at | Time |
| First order at | Minutes from seating — this is your service-latency baseline |
| Re-order times | **The critical field.** Every subsequent order, with its timestamp |
| Bill requested at | |
| Left at | |
| Items observed | Rough — "3 kafe, 1 voda, 2 piva" |
| Payment | Cash / card / phone (IPS) — **this partly closes open item #7**, on which the only existing figure is a lobbying claim |
| Notes | Moved tables? Joined tables? Waiter visits? |

### What to compute

- **Median dwell time**, by daypart and venue band
- **Re-orders per table-hour** — the number W1 exists to increase, and the one nobody has
- **Time from last consumed item to next order** — the gap the ageing indicator is designed to close. If this median is small, W1's premise is weak. If it's 15+ minutes, the wedge is real
- **Table-hours per table per day** and implied turns
- **Cash share of observed payments**
- **Rough average check**, if you can see enough

**Be honest with yourself about the sample.** Ten venues × two sessions is not a study. It is dramatically more than anyone else in this market has, and it's enough to make a build decision and to make a credible claim in a deck, provided you describe the method when you use it.

## 5. Recording and synthesis

**Per interview, same day, one page:** venue profile (type, venues, covers, terrace size, current POS, monthly spend) · the Friday narrative in their words · the last-failure story · task timings and where they hesitated · the W1 verdict (pass/fail/ambiguous, with the actual quote) · their stated blocker to trying · pilot interest (yes/maybe/no) · referrals · **new vocabulary**.

**After all twelve, produce four artifacts:**

1. **A re-ranked wedge list** — with the count of operators who raised each pain unprompted next to it. Unprompted mentions are the currency; prompted agreement is worthless.
2. **The dwell/turnover dataset** with the computed figures. Save it as a project doc. It's a durable asset and it will end up in the deck.
3. **A pricing reality table** — what each venue actually pays today, all-in, versus the €34 headline anchor. You'll almost certainly find the real number is higher and less transparent than published price lists suggest.
4. **A vocabulary list** for UI copy.

**Then update the research report's open items** — #7, #8, #9, #10, #11 should all be closed or materially narrowed. That's the point of the round.

## 6. Things that will go wrong, and what to do

**They talk about price immediately.** Deflect once: *"Doći ćemo do toga — prvo hoću da vidim da li vam ovo uopšte treba."* Price interest before product interest is usually politeness, not buying signal.

**They love everything.** Serbian hospitality is hospitable; you will get warmth that isn't data. Push: *"Šta je najgore ovde? Nešto mora da vas nervira."* If you get no criticism at all, downweight the entire interview.

**They ask for a feature you can't build.** Write it down, say "zapisao sam", move on. Don't design in the room and don't promise.

**They want it now.** Best problem to have. Take the pilot commitment in writing, with a start date, and tell them the truth about certification timing. Over-promising a date you'll miss costs you the reference.

**The grey economy comes up.** It will. Someone will ask, directly or obliquely, whether the system can print a bill without fiscalizing. **Have your answer ready and give it the same way every time:** the system prints a *predračun* before payment, and issues a fiscal receipt at payment, and that's it. Don't moralise, don't negotiate, and don't get drawn into a discussion of what competitors allow. Then note it — because that answer just told you they're not your segment, and knowing your segmentation is one of the round's real outputs.

**Nobody reacts to W1.** That's the round working. See §3.
